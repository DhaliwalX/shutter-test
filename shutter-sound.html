Shutter Sound

Measures shutter speed from an audio recording, either of the shutter’s clicks or of a light sensor plugged into the phone’s mic input.

Recording

Records uncompressed audio with echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto gain turned off.

How to record

Light sensor (accurate to 1/4000)

  1. Solder a photodiode, phototransistor or small solar cell to a 3.5 mm TRRS plug: one lead to the mic contact (the sleeve-side ring), the other to ground.
  2. Connect it through a USB-C to 3.5 mm adapter that passes a mic signal, and record with Voice Memos or any recorder app.
  3. Tape the sensor over the film gate with the back open, point the lens at a bright, steady light, and fire.
  4. Move the sensor to the top, middle and bottom of the gate and compare readings at 1/1000 and faster to check curtain travel.

Clicks (rough, slow speeds)

  1. Hold the phone’s microphone close to the camera in a quiet room.
  2. Fire a few times at each speed, leaving a second or two between firings.
  3. Zoom into each firing and mark the opening and closing sounds yourself if the automatic pairing picks the wrong pair. On an SLR the mirror makes its own clicks, so this method works best from 1/30 to several seconds.
